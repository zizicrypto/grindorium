Grief

Grief is the emotional and psychological response to loss. It arises not only from death but from any significant loss: the end of a relationship, the loss of a job or identity, a diagnosis, a transition that closes off a version of the future you had imagined.

The stages of grief model, while widely known, is frequently misapplied. The stages were not designed as a linear sequence through which everyone passes. Grief is not a process with a finish line. It is a response that changes over time but may never fully resolve, and does not need to.

Grief needs room, time, and the absence of pressure to be over. What it does not need is acceleration, suppression, or the social expectation that a person should be done with it by a certain point.

Complicated grief, grief that persists intensely and interferes significantly with daily function, benefits from professional support. But most grief, while painful and long, is not pathological. It is proportional to what was lost.

The presence of grief does not mean you are not recovering. It means you loved something real.
