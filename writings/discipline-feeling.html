Discipline is not about feeling like it.

You are waiting to feel like it. Waiting for the motivation to arrive, for the resistance to drop, for the right conditions to appear. They do not come. The work does not get done.

Discipline is not the ability to act when you feel motivated. That is easy. Discipline is acting when you do not feel like it, when it is uncomfortable, when the outcome is uncertain, when part of you would rather do almost anything else.

The feeling of wanting to do something is not required for doing it. This is obvious when said plainly. But most people structure their entire relationship to effort around waiting for the feeling first.

The feeling often comes after starting. Rarely before. The resistance is loudest at the beginning and quietest once you are in motion.

You do not have to want to. You just have to start. And starting, repeatedly, in the absence of feeling, is what discipline actually is.
