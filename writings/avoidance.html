Avoidance

Avoidance is the behavioral tendency to stay away from situations, thoughts, emotions, or experiences that produce discomfort. It is one of the most effective short-term strategies for reducing anxiety, and one of the most reliable long-term maintainers of it.

Avoidance works because it produces immediate relief. The anxiety drops. The situation was not faced. The discomfort was avoided. This relief reinforces the avoidance, making it more likely to recur.

The problem is that avoidance prevents the experience of the feared situation going okay. Every avoided situation confirms the implicit belief that the situation was too dangerous to face. The fear is maintained and often grows.

Avoidance takes many forms: behavioral avoidance of situations, cognitive avoidance of thoughts or memories, emotional avoidance of feelings, and safety behaviors, things done within a feared situation to prevent the feared outcome.

Breaking the avoidance cycle requires gradual, supported exposure to what is being avoided, learning through experience that the feared outcome either does not occur or is manageable if it does.
