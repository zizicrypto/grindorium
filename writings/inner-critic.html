Inner Critic

The inner critic is the internalized voice of judgment that evaluates behavior, appearance, and worth, typically in harsh, absolute, and unforgiving terms. It sounds like the voice of honest self-assessment. It is not.

The inner critic is learned. It is constructed from criticism absorbed in early development, from environments where conditional approval taught that performance was required for acceptance, and from cultural messages about what is acceptable and what is not.

The content of the inner critic tends to be global (you are fundamentally inadequate), absolute (you always fail), and focused on identity rather than behavior. These are the features that distinguish it from constructive self-evaluation, which tends to be specific, accurate, and actionable.

The inner critic is often loudest at moments of genuine possibility, before attempting something meaningful, after a mistake, or when something good starts to happen. This is because its function is protective: keeping you small keeps you safe from the risk of failure or visibility.

Working with the inner critic involves learning to recognize its voice as distinct from genuine assessment, understanding what it is protecting against, and gradually building a relationship with self-evaluation that is honest without being punishing.
