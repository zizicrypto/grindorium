Dopamine Dysregulation

Dopamine is a neurotransmitter central to the brain's reward and motivation systems. It is released in anticipation of reward and plays a key role in reinforcing behaviors, not primarily producing pleasure, but driving the seeking behavior that precedes it.

Dopamine dysregulation occurs when the reward system is chronically overstimulated, by high-stimulation content, substances, or behaviors designed to exploit the dopamine system. The result is a recalibration that makes ordinary levels of stimulation feel insufficient.

Symptoms of dopamine dysregulation include difficulty sustaining attention on unstimulating tasks, reduced motivation for activities that do not provide immediate reward, inability to tolerate boredom, and a persistent sense that ordinary life is flat or insufficient.

The modern digital environment is specifically designed to exploit the dopamine system. Variable reward schedules, the unpredictability of what will appear next in a feed, are among the most powerful drivers of seeking behavior.

Recovery involves periods of deliberate low stimulation that allow the system to recalibrate. Boredom, which feels intolerable during dysregulation, is an important part of the recovery process.
