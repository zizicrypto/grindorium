Summer Pressure

Summer arrives with an unwritten assignment: be visibly happy now. The weather is good, the days are long, everyone's feed fills with beaches and rooftops and golden-hour laughter, and a quiet question starts following you around. Why isn't your life like that. In winter, staying in is just weather. In summer, staying in feels like evidence.

This is summer pressure, the seasonal version of falling short. It works because summer carries a cultural script older than social media, summers from childhood, summers from movies, the idea that these months are when life is supposed to happen. Feeds did not invent the script. They industrialized it. Now the best afternoon of three hundred strangers arrives in your hand while you are eating lunch alone at your desk.

The comparison is rigged in a specific way. You are comparing your unedited interior, the boredom, the loneliness, the ordinary Tuesday, against other people's curated exteriors. Nobody posts the hour before the picture or the argument after it. You know this. Knowing it does not help much, because the comparison happens below the level where knowing operates. The image lands first. The correction limps in afterward.

For people already lonely, the season is heavier. Winter loneliness can pass as coziness. Summer loneliness has nowhere to hide. The long bright evenings that are supposed to be the reward of the year become the hardest hours to fill, and the gap between the season's promise and your actual evening becomes a measurement you take daily.

There is also the strange grief of watching a summer pass unused. Each one is countable. Some part of you keeps an inventory, and an August evening can deliver a very specific ache: another one ending, and where was I. That ache is real and worth respecting, because under the pressure there is an actual signal. You do want aliveness. The script is wrong about what aliveness looks like, not about whether it matters.

What helps is shrinking the unit of comparison. Not your summer against the season's highlight reel, but this evening against nothing at all. One swim. One walk after dark when the heat breaks. One meal eaten outside. Aliveness in real life is small and frequent, not cinematic and rare, and a summer made of thirty small good moments will be remembered more warmly than one staged weekend. If the feed makes the measuring worse, and it does, less feed during these months is not avoidance. It is removing a distorted ruler.

You are not behind on summer. The season is not a performance with attendance taken. It is three months of long light, and they belong as much to a quiet porch as to anyone's boat.
