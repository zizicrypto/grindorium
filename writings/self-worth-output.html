Your worth is not your output.

At some point, doing became the measure of being. Your value began to feel proportional to your output. Rest became something to earn. A quiet day became evidence of falling behind.

This is not an accident. It is a learned equation. And like most learned equations, it feels like truth until you examine it.

The problem with making worth conditional on output is that output is variable. Bad days become personal failures. Limitations become character flaws. The relentless drive to produce is not ambition. It is anxiety with a to-do list.

You are not the sum of what you produce. You were worth something before you were useful to anyone. You will be worth something after you can no longer be useful to anyone.

The work matters. But the work is not you. And when those two things get confused, the work suffers too, because it is carrying something it was never designed to hold.
