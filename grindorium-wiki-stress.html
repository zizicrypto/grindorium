Stress

Not all stress is the same. Understanding the difference between useful stress and chronic stress changes how you respond to both.

What is stress?

Stress is the physiological and psychological response to demands that exceed perceived resources. It is not inherently harmful, in the short term, stress activates systems that improve performance, focus, and resilience. The problem is chronic stress: sustained activation without adequate recovery.

Acute vs. chronic stress

Acute stress

Short-term, specific, and often useful. The stress of a deadline, a difficult conversation, or a physical challenge. It activates, then passes. Recovery is part of the cycle.

Chronic stress

Sustained over weeks or months. The body stays in a state of activation without completing the stress cycle. This is where the damage accumulates, to the cardiovascular system, immune function, cognitive capacity, and emotional regulation.

The stress response

When the brain perceives threat, it activates the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, releasing cortisol and adrenaline. Heart rate increases. Blood flow shifts toward large muscles. Digestion slows. Attention narrows to the immediate threat.

This is precisely the right response to a physical threat. It becomes problematic when activated repeatedly by psychological stressors that never fully resolve.

Signs your stress load is too high

  • Persistent physical tension that does not fully release
  • Sleep disruption, difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking restored
  • Irritability disproportionate to the triggering situation
  • Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
  • Getting sick more frequently
  • Feeling overwhelmed even when the situation is manageable
  • Using substances, food, or screens to numb rather than recover

What stress actually needs

The stress cycle requires completion. Physical activity, emotional expression, rest, and connection all help complete stress cycles that otherwise stay open. The problem is not just the stressor, it is the incomplete response.

Understanding where your stress comes from matters as much as managing the response. Stress that comes from values conflict, lack of control, or insufficient recovery needs different responses than stress from workload alone.
